Across all three missions, the thing that makes a route suitable for a robot sharing space with people is the route quality and if the route maintains human safety as a priority by keeping the minimum clearance distance between the robot and the person high and making sure that the robot is in a person's vicinity for the least amount of time possible. Search efficiency for a route is important so that latency isn't a problem because a the robot determining a path after the environment might have changed  may lead to an invalid path, but without route quality, the generated route wouldn't be good even with search efficiency since the route wouldn't be useable as it may cause harm to people or might increase distance to travel. For example, in mission 1, on the open map, DFS had better search efficiency than BFS with the planning time for DFS being 0.0005 s with 558 expanded nodes and the planning time for BFS being 0.0008 s with 928 expanded nodes. However, BFS had better route quality with the shortest path length of 13.5 while DFS had a path length of 111. Since route quality may differ on different maps, the planner for generating the map matters since this can affect navigation with the route possibly causing the robot to travel more distance or to have to make tighter turns. For example, in mission 2, although BFS generated the shortest path for both the open and constrained map, the robot had to make 4 tight turns on the constrained map because of the shape of the map compared to only one tight turn on the open map. Social field tradeoffs like taking the shortest possible path versus potentially staying closer to people and endangering them also affect whether the route is suitable because with people around, selecting a longer path, but ensuring human safety is usually more important. For example in mission 3, RRT social_1 was the best route recommendation out of all the trials because it had the largest minimum person clearance of 1.7274 m which meant more space between the robot and person and it was in the personal space of the person for 0 s which meant that it was never below the threshold distance. However, these estimates of the specific navigation of the robot for tight turns while following the path and the minimum person clearances  between the robot and person are not the ground truth because the specific movement the robot may perform and the resulting amount of clearance between the robot and person may differ from trial to trial even if the same route is used.
