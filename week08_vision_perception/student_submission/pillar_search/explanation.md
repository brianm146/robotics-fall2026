# pillar_search

## diagnosis
The saved attempt "20261030T214241-4845e122" was successfully centered on the blue pillar. When the robot started, the camera image was not detecting blue pixels so the robot continued to turn left until it detected blue pixels with the mask converting a central region of the camera image into HSV, thresholding the color blue to displaying as white, and selects the largest contour being the blue pillar. After centering and holding the image, the centroid error was almost 0 being 0.0024. By changing the steering gain setting from 1.20 to 1.30, the robot improved its image centering from having the image center being detected off to the right of the centroid to having the image center being detected almost directly on top of the centroid.

## evidence
For my four-color attempt "20261030T221322-a48258fc", the colors red, green, and turquoise were centered, but the color blue was not centered. The state timeline shows that for each color, the camera kept turning left slowly, searching for that specific color, then oscillated a little while trying to the center the image on that pillar, then confirmed the detected center with three different centered images, held the robot with zero speed for some time on the centered image for that color, and then moved to the next image. The robot controller was completed after the final blue pillar was processed. The camera evidence showed that the colors red, green, and turquoise were centered  with small errors of -0.00003, 0.007, and 0.0012, respectively. A centered colored region and a stable hold does not prove object identity or distance because the camera simply detects the object with those color pixels which doesn't ensure that it is the correct object and being able to detect the centered colored region only means that the object is in range of the robot to capture the image, but doesn't tell you the distance of the object from the robot.
