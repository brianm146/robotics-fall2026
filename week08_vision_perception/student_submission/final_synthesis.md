The three missions taught me that turning visual evidence into reliable robot behavior can be difficult because the visual evidence can sometimes not be accurate in different conditions or scenarios. For example, in the classical and learned detector comparison, the classical detector failed to detect the mug in the dim_1 image while being able to detect it in the normal_1 image since in the dim condition, most of the pixels on the mug don't fall into those specific settings with value/brightness set from 25 to 70 and the learned detector was not able to detect the mug at all when it was occluded. The perception choice of setting value/brightness from 25 to 180 to 25 to 180 for the classical detector and the perception choice of changing the learned acceptance threshold to 0.25 for the learned detector made the robot detect the mug easier in more conditions, but the classical detector was still limited in its ability to detect the mug in certain conditions like dim_1 and the learned detector was still limited in its ability to detect mugs in the occluded condition. For pillar centering, in the four-color attempt, the higher steering gain to 1.50 was able to correctly center the red, green, and turquoise colors, but still failed to center the blue pillar while also having some oscillation when trying to center the pillars. For the line-following experiments, in the track attempts, there was high error and lots of oscillation even with a higher gain of 0.35 because the curved shape of the track made the steering unstable. For both pillar centering and the line-following experiments, the control choice I made was increasing the gain which made the robot center onto the pillars and magenta line better, but also limited it with more oscillation.
