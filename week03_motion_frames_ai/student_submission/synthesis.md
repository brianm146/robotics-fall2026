# Technical synthesis

One example of a robot motion program being technically correct and still failing as a part of a sociotechnical system is that the robot doesn't account for pedestrian actions. For instance, if the robot is performing a certain movement pattern that may not be entirely predictable, but doesn't include any way of gesturing or signaling to the user of this, the robot may accidentally hit the person or cause the person to mistrust the robot. Additionally, even though the robot may fit certain speed constraints which are technically correct, these speeds may not allowed pedestrians an adequate amount of time to react to the robot depending on the demographic of the  pedestrian, like whether they are an active young person or whether they are someone in wheelchair who might need slower robot speeds.
